\begin{abstract}
Marginal-preserving rollout dependence changes which successful and unsuccessful
trajectories are contrasted by group-relative learning. We study this effect at the
on-policy score point. A partner operator characterizes the expected RLOO update,
and invariance of the policy score subspace characterizes when dependence acts as
a reward-independent positive natural preconditioner. Stratified sampling induces
bounded reward-space reweighting, whereas lattice sampling can suppress reward
modes. For binary rewards, an exact decomposition separates success-count weighting
from response-identity selection, including population-standardized score-point
advantages. Integrating count-preserving rewiring yields a deterministic intervention
on identical stored trajectories. Independent IID data also yield stratified-update
forecasts. Finite-policy enumeration reproduces two reversal examples and validates
the normalized forecast. The accompanying experimental protocol tests these predictions
on unrestricted pretrained-model responses. This initial manuscript reports theory
and CPU validation; pretrained-model effects and learning consequences remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}
